## What I can share

Enterprise work behind an NDA: outcomes and architecture altitude only.

## The work

bp's supply-chain platform moves the data and transactions behind physical energy logistics.
I built and maintained the automation layer: pipelines that processed operational data and services that executed transactional logic that people previously pushed through by hand.

The automation program I contributed to returned roughly $5M per year in operational efficiencies - the kind of number that only survives when the underlying system is boring in the best sense: typed contracts between services, idempotent processing, observability from day one, and careful rollout behind flags.

Later in the engagement, that platform work extended into four production sub-agents automating slices of the supply-chain workflow.
Working at that scale set the bar I still design against: an automation is only done when the people accountable for the process can see what it did and why.

## Why it matters

Enterprise supply chains are unforgiving hosts for automation - the data is messy, the stakes are financial, and trust is earned in production, not in demos.
This is where I learned to treat auditability and human accountability as load-bearing features, which is exactly the philosophy the public case studies on this site demonstrate in the open.
